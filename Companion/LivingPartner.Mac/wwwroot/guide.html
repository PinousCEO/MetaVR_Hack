Your first session with Nilo

Connect your desk

  1. Open Yonder Companion on Windows, or open Start-Yonder.command on Mac. On Mac, move the browser to the screen you want to use and choose Use this display.
  2. Open Yonder on Quest. Keep the headset and computer on the same local network. Enter the companion’s six-digit code if asked. Allow microphone and camera access for voice and recognition.
  3. Look at the monitor when it lights up. If you move it later, select Locate screen again on Windows or Calibrate TV on Mac.

Try the crossing first

Point at your calibrated monitor and say “Hey, Nilo, enter the screen.” Watch where his feet cross the glass. Say “Hey, Nilo, come back.” to bring him back into your room.

Work together

  • Copy a cup. Keep the cup visible, point to it, and say “Hey, Nilo, copy this cup.” Find the finished item in your bag. Ask Nilo to put it on your desk or export it as a GLB for your computer.
  • Read a screen. Point to the window you mean before asking what it says. On Mac, allow screen recording for screen-based requests.
  • Make a tool on Windows. Ask for a Meta stock chart. Follow its progress in Workshop, open the finished tool, and try selecting a chart button by pointing and pinching. Open the marketplace to share modules.

Play across the screen

Ask Nilo to play tennis and swing to return his serve. In goalkeeper, reach into the path of the shot. For two-player Gomoku, both players watch the same TV. Keep the guest’s pointing hand and the board in the headset’s view during their turn.

If Nilo stops responding

  • After waking Quest: bring Yonder back to the foreground and check whether the companion shows a connected headset. Keep the companion running. If a code appears, pair again.
  • Voice: check microphone permission on the headset. On Windows try the tray’s chat page. On Mac use Talk to Nilo in TV settings. If text works, check the microphone before recalibrating.
  • Creation: open the job to read its status. Physical-object generation requires a Tripo key. Other AI-assisted requests can require OpenAI or Anthropic credentials. See the service setup guide.
